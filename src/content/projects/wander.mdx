problem: "Travel inspiration lives in feeds, group chats, and screenshots. The same ten spots get saved everywhere, and the useful recs never make it onto a map you can actually use."
approach: "Wander puts saved places, friend recommendations, and official destination maps in one product. Links from social posts can land as pins; planning is about days and routes, not a notes app full of names you will forget."
results: "The live product is a map-first travel app: import from common social and web sources, ask Wander AI for nearby ideas, follow friends' saves, and download maps for offline use."
lessons: "A map is only useful if getting a place onto it is cheaper than taking a screenshot. Import and social graph matter more than another pretty pin style."
next: "I would keep the path from 'I saw this' to 'it is on my map' as the main loop, and I would be stricter about which surfaces need native work versus what can stay in the map UI."

[Wander](https://www.wandermaps.com/) is a travel product built around a simple claim: the world is bigger than your feed. It collects places from friends, from destination stewards, and from links you paste, then lets you plan days and take the map offline.
